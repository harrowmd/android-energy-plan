Energy Plan – User Guide

Energy Plan sketches simple floor plans for home energy surveys. You draw rectangular rooms, type their exact sizes, mark what each wall is, and save the plan as A4 pages (JPG and PDF) plus a data file (JSON) with every area, volume and wall length worked out for you.

This guide covers version 1.8.1 and later. The latest copy is on GitHub: https://github.com/harrowmd/android-energy-plan/blob/main/USERGUIDE.md

1. The screen

  • Toolbar (top): the plan name and the floor you're looking at, then
    • Floor button (e.g. GROUND) – switch floors. On a narrow phone it moves into the ⋮ menu as Floor: Ground floor…
    • Undo – take back the last change.
    • Fit – zoom so the whole floor fits the screen.
    • Save – save the plan (see Saving).
    • ⋮ – the menu with everything else.
  • Plan (middle): a grid with 50 cm squares (shown bottom-left, e.g. Grid 50 cm · snap 10 cm). Rooms are pale green; walls are solid bars in their type's colour; the floor below shows as faint dashed outlines.
  • Status (bottom): with nothing selected, the number of rooms, area and volume of the floor shown, and a reminder of the gestures. With a room selected, its name, building part, sizes, area and volume.
  • Buttons (bottom): + Room (add a room by typing its sizes), Edit and Delete (for the selected room).

2. Touch gestures

Gesture Where What it does
Drag one finger Anywhere Pans the plan (or draws a room, if One finger create room is on)
Pinch / two fingers Anywhere Zooms and pans
Long-press Inside a room Selects it (top room where rooms overlap); keep holding and drag to move it
Drag The selected room Moves it; edges snap to other rooms and the 10 cm grid
Tap Empty space Clears the selection
Double-tap Inside a room Opens the room's dialog (name, floor, part, sizes)
Double-tap On a wall Opens the wall dialog (type and thickness)
Long-press On an outside wall Adds a bay window there
Long-press On a bay window Changes or removes it

A short tap on a room does not select it, so rooms aren't picked by accident. A short vibration tells you a long-press has worked.

3. Rooms

Adding a room

  • + Room: type a name, choose its floor and building part, and enter

    • Width – x (left to right, metres),
    • Depth – y (top to bottom, metres),
    • Ceiling height – z (metres).

    It's placed to the right of the rooms already on that floor, touching them. Drag it into place.

  • Draw with one finger (optional): turn on ⋮ → Settings… → One finger create room, then drag on the grid. Corners snap to nearby room edges or the 10 cm grid; when you lift your finger, the room dialog opens with the drawn size filled in.

Sizes are internal (inside the walls) – measure wall face to wall face. The ceiling height you last typed is used for the next new room, and new rooms start on the floor shown and in the building part you used last.

Moving, editing and deleting

  • Move: long-press the room and drag, or drag it once selected. Edges snap to the edges of other rooms (on any floor) and to a 10 cm grid, so neighbouring rooms line up exactly.
  • Edit: double-tap the room, or select it and tap Edit. You can change its name, floor, building part and sizes. Changing the floor moves the view to that floor.
  • Delete: select it and tap Delete, then choose
    • Delete completely – the room and its walls go; or
    • Make unallocated – keep the space and its outside walls, but it's no longer a room (see Unallocated space).

What's shown in a room

The room's name, its net floor area, its height (and its building part if it isn't Main), with the width along the top wall and the depth along the left wall. Labels move to the visible part of a room if another room covers part of it, and keep clear of thick walls.

4. Walls

Automatic walls

Every side of every room gets a wall automatically:

  • Where another room on the same floor touches it (or is within 15 cm), that stretch is a shared Internal wall, drawn centred on the join – half in each room – so the rooms stay neatly aligned.
  • Everything else is External and is drawn outside the room, wrapping round it, with square corners.

Draw rooms touching each other – snapping makes this easy – and the walls take care of themselves.

Wall types

Type Default thickness Default colour Typical use
External 350 mm Red Outside walls
Internal 120 mm Green Walls between rooms
Sheltered 150 mm Purple Walls to an unheated space (garage, loft, store)
Party 300 mm Orange Walls shared with a neighbouring home
Stud 100 mm Blue Timber stud partitions
Glazing 80 mm Pink Fully glazed walls (e.g. conservatories)

Changing a wall

Double-tap a wall. The wall is outlined and a dialog shows where it is (e.g. Between Kitchen and Dining · 3.50 m long) and its automatic type.

  • Pick a type – its default thickness is filled in.
  • Change the Thickness (mm) if the wall is thicker or thinner.
  • OK saves it; Automatic puts the wall back to its automatic type.

A wall is split into separate pieces wherever the room on the other side changes, and each piece keeps its own setting. On an outside wall, all the outside stretches on one side of a room share one setting.

Wall colours can be changed in ⋮ → Settings….

5. Bay windows

Long-press an outside wall where you want the bay. Choose:

  • Type:
    • Angled – a front parallel to the wall with 45° sides (steeper if the bay is too deep for 45°).
    • Square – a rectangular box.
    • Bow – a circular arc.
  • Depth out from the wall (metres).
  • Width along the wall (metres) – the dialog shows the most that fits on that stretch of wall.

The bay is centred where you pressed, within that stretch of wall; bays on the same wall can't overlap.

What a bay does:

  • Its floor area is added to the room (and the room's volume).
  • The wall follows the bay's outline, with the same type and thickness as the wall it's on. That wall gets longer by the bay's outline less its width, which increases the HLP (or PWL for a party wall).
  • Bays belong to their floor: Add floor and Add basement don't copy them.

Long-press a bay to change it, or tap Remove in its dialog.

In the room schedule, rooms with bays are marked †, because their area is more than width × depth.

6. Floors

Each room is on a floor: Basement, Ground, 1st … 7th.

  • Switch floors: tap the floor button in the toolbar (or ⋮ → Floor:… on a phone). The list shows how many rooms each floor has. Switching keeps the same zoom and position, so floors line up as you flip between them.
  • Guide outlines: the nearest other floor with rooms (the one below, where there is one) is drawn as faint dashed outlines, so you can line up the rooms above. Room edges snap to them too.
  • New rooms go on the floor shown. To move a room to another floor, change Floor in its dialog.
  • Rooms on different floors never overlap or share walls.

Add floor and Add basement

To copy a whole floor's layout as a starting point:

  1. Long-press any room in the building part you want to copy (e.g. Main).
  2. ⋮ → Add floor: copy Main to 1st floor.
  3. Confirm. Every room in that building part on that floor is copied to the floor above, with the same sizes, positions and wall changes (but not bay windows). The view moves to the new floor.
  4. Rename, resize, delete or make rooms unallocated as needed.

From the ground floor, ⋮ → Add basement does the same downwards. One Undo removes a whole copy. If the target floor already has rooms in that part, you're warned first; the copies are added on top.

7. Building parts

Each room belongs to a building part: Main, Ext1 to Ext4 (extensions) or Conservatory. Choose it in the room dialog. Rooms outside Main show their part on the plan (e.g. h 2.40 m · Ext1).

Areas, volumes, HLP and PWL are given for each part and floor in the Summary.

8. Overlapping rooms

A room drawn later sits on top of earlier ones. Where rooms overlap, the overlap belongs to the room on top: the room underneath loses that area (and volume), and the totals don't count it twice. For example, draw a large room and then a WC inside it – the large room's area excludes the WC.

Change which room is on top from the ⋮ menu (select a room first):

  • Bring forward / Send backward – one step past the next room it overlaps (rooms it doesn't touch are skipped).
  • Bring to front / Send to back – all the way.

Items that would change nothing are greyed out. In the room schedule, rooms that lose area to a room on top are marked *.

9. Unallocated space

Delete → Make unallocated turns a room into unallocated space – for example a loft void, stairwell, or the parts of a floor copied with Add floor that don't exist upstairs.

  • It's shown as grey hatching only (no name, sizes or outline). The floor below shows through as dashed outlines.
  • It adds no area or volume to any total.
  • Its outside walls stay – they're still the building's envelope and count towards HLP and PWL (for its building part and floor).
  • There's no wall between two unallocated spaces. A room next to unallocated space keeps its wall (Internal by default – change it to Sheltered if the space is unheated).
  • Select it and tap Delete again to Delete completely or Restore as room (it gets its name back).

10. Summary: area, height, HLP and PWL

⋮ → Summary shows a table for the whole plan:

Column Meaning
Building part, Floor One row per building part on each floor
Area m² Net floor area of the rooms (including bays, excluding unallocated space and parts covered by rooms on top)
Height m Average ceiling height, weighted by area
HLP m Heat loss perimeter: total length of External, Sheltered and Glazing walls
PWL m Party wall length: total length of Party walls

A part on more than one floor gets a subtotal row, and the last row is the total. All wall lengths are measured along the inside face of the rooms.

The same table appears in the saved files: under each floor's drawing (that floor's rows) and as the Summary page (with subtotals).

11. Saving, files and opening plans

Save

Tap Save, check the plan name, and tap Save. The app writes, named with today's date and the plan name (spaces become _):

File What's in it
2026-10-18-My_House.Ground.jpg – one per floor (.Basement, .Ground, .1st, …) That floor on an A4 page: the drawing, a 1 m scale bar and the floor's dimensions table
2026-10-18-My_House.Summary.jpg (and .Summary-2.jpg … if longer) The summary (dimensions with subtotals), the wall schedule (total length of each wall type and thickness) and the room schedule (every room with floor, part, sizes, area and volume, plus totals per floor and per part)
2026-10-18-My_House.pdf All of the pages above, at A4 – ready to print or email
2026-10-18-My_House.json The plan data, to open again later or use elsewhere
2026-10-18-My_House.txt The app's log, which helps if something goes wrong
  • Floor pages are landscape if the building is wider than it is deep. All floors use the same scale and position, so they line up page to page.
  • Saving again on the same day replaces that day's files (and removes page JPGs no longer needed, e.g. a floor you deleted). Saving on another day makes a new set, so earlier days are kept.
  • Only one save runs at a time; pressing Save during a save shows Still saving….

Where files go – Save location

By default, JPGs go to Pictures/EnergyPlan (they appear in the gallery) and everything else to Documents/EnergyPlan.

⋮ → Save location… → Choose folder… sends all files to a folder you pick instead – including a cloud folder such as Nextcloud, if its app offers one in the picker. Use default goes back to the default folders. Saving to a cloud folder is slower (each file is uploaded; allow 20–40 seconds).

Open a saved plan

⋮ → Open plan… and pick a saved .json file. The current plan can be brought back with Undo.

Rename

⋮ → Rename plan… changes the plan name (used in the file names and on the pages).

Share log

⋮ → Share log sends the app's log (e.g. by email) – useful when reporting a problem.

12. Undo, autosave and new plans

  • Undo reverses the last change – adding, editing, moving, deleting, restacking, wall and bay changes, copying floors – up to 50 steps.
  • The plan on screen is saved automatically after every change, so closing the app (or updating it) loses nothing. Autosave is separate from Save: it doesn't write the JPG/PDF/JSON files.
  • ⋮ → New plan starts an empty plan after asking you to confirm. The old one can still be brought back with Undo (or opened from its saved JSON).

13. Settings

⋮ → Settings…:

  • One finger create room – No (default): one finger pans the plan. Yes: one-finger drag draws a new room. It's off by default because it's easy to create rooms by accident.
  • Wall colours – one entry per wall type. Pick from 16 colours, enter a custom #RRGGBB colour, or go back to the Default. Colours are used on screen and in the saved pages. They're a setting on each device, not part of the plan.

14. Updating the app

⋮ → About shows the version and build date, with:

  • Check for update – asks GitHub for the latest release. If it's newer, you see what's new and can Download and install it.
  • Reinstall – downloads this same version again from GitHub and installs it over the app (useful if something seems wrong with the installed copy).

The first time, Android asks you to allow Energy Plan to install apps (Install unknown apps → Allow from this source). Turn it on, come back to the app and tap the button again; Android's installer then asks you to confirm. The plan on screen and your settings are kept.

16. Tips and troubleshooting

  • Work room by room, touching: add a room, drag it against its neighbour until it snaps, then the next. Shared walls appear automatically.
  • Copy floors: draw the ground floor completely, then use Add floor and adjust, rather than drawing upper floors from scratch.
  • Unheated spaces: make a room unallocated (or leave it out), then set the walls of the heated rooms facing it to Sheltered.
  • A room won't select: use a long-press, not a tap. Where rooms overlap the top one is selected; use Send backward to reach one underneath.
  • A wall dialog opens instead of the room's: you double-tapped close to a wall; double-tap nearer the middle of the room.
  • Long-press on a wall between rooms says "Bay windows go on outside walls": bays can only be added to outside walls.
  • Saving to a cloud folder is slow: that's the upload; wait for the Saved message. Use the default folders for speed.
  • Something looks wrong: ⋮ → Share log and send the log with a description of what happened.

17. Data file (JSON) reference

The .json file holds everything needed to reopen the plan, plus the calculated results:

  • name, saved, units (m), formatVersion.
  • rooms – in stacking order (bottom first). Each has id, name, floor, buildingPart, unallocated, x, y (top-left corner), width, depth, height, grossArea, area (net), volume, and bays (type, side, offset, width, depth, area, perimeter) if it has any.
  • walls – every stretch of wall: floor, roomId, room, side, neighbourId, neighbour (null for outside walls), type, thickness, length (including bays) and its end points.
  • wallTotals – total length per wall type and thickness.
  • dimensions and dimensionsTotal – the Summary table: area, height, HLP and PWL per building part and floor.
  • floorTotals, partTotals, totalArea, totalVolume, unallocatedArea.
  • wallOverrides – the walls you changed (the only wall data read back when opening a plan; everything else is recalculated).

All lengths are in metres and areas in m².
